update on training the RoMA V2 matcher from scratch (4 CO3D categories, 12k steps) with the matching losses plus a reconstruction decoder on its output tokens — scratch-joint. The headline holds at our largest scale: the joint objective keeps full appearance in the features at no cost to correspondence.

Main results

Correspondence — coarse matcher (held-out, 320 px eval, 209 pairs; attention-argmax EPE, px). Shown against the released pretrained RoMA checkpoint as a reference, coarse matcher only (its learned refinement stack, which our from-scratch model doesn’t have, is excluded):

Near-view coarse matching is on par with the pretrained model (~6 px). The remaining gap is purely wide-baseline — the pretrained prior still leads on far pairs (9.2 vs 15.3 px) — and it closes as we scale.

Appearance retained in the features. A frozen-feature reconstruction probe recovers 19.1 dB PSNR from the jointly trained tokens — right at the 19.0 dB ceiling of its frozen DINOv3 inputs — while the standard pretrained matcher has collapsed to 13.3 dB. Training the matcher with the reconstruction objective present from step 0 simply prevents that collapse.

Reconstruction-probe PSNR: the from-scratch joint model sits at the frozen-DINOv3 input ceiling; the pretrained matcher is collapsed.

Ablation — frozen-DINOv3 baseline

To isolate what the trained deep encoder contributes, we ran the same recipe (same heads, losses, 12k-step budget) with the trainable encoder replaced by a linear projection of the frozen DINOv3 features — desc-joint. It is a diagnostic control, not a candidate model, and it cleanly separates the two axes:

Matching: the deep encoder’s entire contribution is wide-baseline. Near-view coarse matching is fine without it (frozen features + trained head, ~7 px), but far pairs collapse without a deep encoder — raw projected-DINOv3 similarity cannot do coarse wide-baseline association:

coarse EPE (px) ↓scratch-joint
(deep encoder)		desc-joint
(frozen DINOv3 + linear)
all pairs10.2531.47

far pairs15.2960.65

Reconstruction: appearance is cheap at every depth. The frozen-DINOv3 baseline’s own decoder actually reconstructs held-out views slightly better than the deep model’s (23.1 vs 21.4 dB) — largely because it fits a static target instead of chasing a still-training encoder. So the collapse we started from is not caused by reconstruction being hard; it needs a deep encoder trained without a reconstruction objective — exactly the standard recipe.

Held-out reconstruction PSNR (each model’s own decoder, 237 eval pairs): the frozen-DINOv3 baseline (blue) reconstructs on par with / above the deep joint model (green) — appearance is not the bottleneck, wide-baseline correspondence is.
